Who does what

Three voices shape every piece of work — from customer idea to delivered value

Sponsor / Stakeholders
Voice of the Customer
Idea management / Problem definition (Zendesk)
• Defines the problem space — surfaces the customer or business pain, not a pre-built solution
• Owns and prioritizes Initiatives and champions Epics
• Sets the desired outcomes and acceptance criteria at the Initiative and Epic level
• Validates that delivered value resolves the original problem
Product / BA
The Ultimate Enabler
Backlog management (ADO)
• Partners with the Sponsor to ensure the problem space is fully understood and outcomes are clearly articulated before any work begins
• Shapes a well-defined problem into Features and User Stories the dev team can estimate and execute
• Translates business intent into delivery structure — bridging the Sponsor's outcome goals with the team's sprint work
• Facilitates refinement and writes acceptance criteria so nothing is lost in translation
Development Team
Delivery & Execution
Execution (ADO)
• Estimates, sequences, and executes Stories and Tasks
• Identifies, logs, and resolves Bugs
• Surfaces technical constraints that may reshape a Feature or Epic
• Delivers working software each sprint with traceable ADO history
Ownership clarity
Sponsor → owns Initiative / Epic problem statement · Product / BA → manages backlog items in ADO · Dev Team → executes Stories / Tasks / Bugs

For sponsors & stakeholders

Your voice shapes every initiative — this model protects that

🎯
You define
The problem space
• The customer pain and desired outcome
• What success looks like at the Epic and Initiative level
• Guardrails that keep delivery aligned to your intent
• Acceptance criteria before a Feature ships
This is the most important input in the process.
⚙️
Tech Manages
The backlog & execution
• Translating your problem into Features and Stories in ADO
• Sequencing and estimating sprint work
• Daily technical decisions within the guardrails you set
• Keeping the backlog current and traceable
You don't need to track individual ADO items.
✅
You validate
The outcome
• That what shipped resolves the problem you defined
• Sprint reviews / demos — your regular touchpoint
• Roadmap checkpoints on priorities and strategic direction
• Escalation path when something needs your direct input
Regular cadence. Meaningful moments. Not daily overhead.
The goal of this model: Your customer insight shapes every delivery decision. Having all stakeholders present in every meeting creates delays, conflicting priorities, and real cost. This model keeps you central — through defined guardrails and sprint-cadence touchpoints — while the technology team executes.

Trip sharing example

A FlightBridge Example

Planning Work Items
Initiative: Build connected trip experiences across operators and FBO partners.
Epic: Real-time trip sharing between operators and FBO contacts.
Feature: Operator-initiated live trip share with configurable FBO access.
Feature: FBO-facing view showing inbound flight status and ground service requests.
Delivery Work Items
User story: As an operator, I want to share a live trip link with my FBO so they can see arrival details without calling.
User story: As an FBO, I want to see the inbound flight status and ground needs for an arriving customer on a shared itinerary view.
Tasks: Build sharing API, implement link-based access controls, build FBO read-only view, add real-time status sync, and trigger arrival notifications.

Role clarity

Stakeholders own the customer problem — Product / BA owns the backlog

🏢 Large — multi-team, multi-quarter
Initiative
"We need to completely rethink how operators manage ground logistics across a trip."
🎯 Meaningful — multi-feature outcome
Epic
"Customers want self-service control over hotel and ground changes for a booked trip."
⚙️ Specific — plannable capability
Feature
"Allow users to modify their rental car reservation without calling a CSR."
✨ Small — sprint-sized enhancement
User Story
"As an operator, I want to see the hotel confirmation number on my trip summary page."
🐛 Defect — specific broken behavior
Bug
"Hotel dates are displaying in UTC instead of the operator's local time zone."
💡 Key principle
The same customer conversation can produce items at multiple levels. A Sponsor hears a broad complaint and raises an idea. A BA digs into the detail and shapes it into a Story or Epic. A developer finds a related Bug during testing. All three trace back to that one original idea — where it lands in the hierarchy depends on scope, not on who raised it.
Optional Context · New in 2025

Business Case — anchoring Initiative and Epic definition

A new organizational artifact introduced this year. Teams are still learning the format — this slide is a companion to the Three-Layer Model to help explain where it fits.

What is a Business Case?
A structured artifact that forces clarity on why an investment should be made before committing to delivery. It is created during the strategy and guardrails layers — not during sprint execution.
Where it fits in the hierarchy
Initiative Required — defines the strategic investment thesis
Epic Strongly recommended — scopes the outcome group
Feature Optional — for larger capabilities with significant scope
What a Business Case captures
Problem statement — what customer or business problem are we solving?
Strategic alignment — which Initiative or Theme does this support?
Proposed scope — Epic and Feature-level outcomes expected
Success metrics — how will we know this delivered value?
Investment ask — team, timeline, and level-of-effort estimate
💡 The Business Case is owned by the Sponsor / Stakeholder layer and informed by the Leadership Team. It becomes an input to the BA's refinement process.
